Rules for every sentence:
- Write in the first person, in plain words, one idea per sentence. Say what you were responsible for, what you decided and what came of it. Do not praise yourself and do not flatter the company.
- A sentence about you cites the ids of the evidence it rests on, in "ev". Cite only ids from the evidence list. A sentence that only frames the letter (you apply, you say thanks) has "frame": true and an empty "ev"; it may name only the job and the company.
- Use only facts that the cited evidence states. Do not add tools, products, clients, places, certificates, results or reasons that it does not state. When the evidence is thin, write a shorter sentence instead of filling it out.
- State your part exactly as the evidence states it. When the evidence says you helped, proposed, drafted, took part, or did the work with the team or a colleague, keep that word; never turn it into led, designed, built or owned.
- Keep the names the evidence holds: a client, a product, a program or a place says more than "a customer" or "a cluster". The evidence ends with the names it holds and their spelling in the letter's language when the profile has them; those need no declaration.
