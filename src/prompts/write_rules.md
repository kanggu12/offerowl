Rules for every sentence, in both languages:
- Use only facts that the cited evidence states. Do not add tools, products, clients, places, certificates, programs, results, reasons or numbers that it does not state. When the evidence is thin, write less.
- Every sentence cites, in "ev", the ids of the evidence blocks it rests on (the ids in square brackets). Cite blocks, not whole projects. Cite only ids from the evidence list.
- State the person's part exactly as the evidence states it. When the evidence says the person helped, proposed, drafted, took part, or did the work with the team or a colleague, keep that word ("drafted and applied with a colleague", "proposed a design"). Never turn it into led, designed, built or owned.
- Do not dress up routine work: a handover, a meeting, a report, an upgrade or keeping a system running is a duty, not an achievement, unless the evidence states what came of it.
- Keep the names the evidence holds: clients, products, programs, places and tools. A sentence with the client's name says more than "a client". Write each name the usual way in each language; when your spelling differs from the evidence (an English name for a Korean one, or the reverse), add it to "names": {"your spelling": "the exact text from the evidence"}.
- Use the terms the industry uses. Keep a tool or method name in English when the evidence writes it in English, in the Korean text too. Do not coin a word-for-word translation.
